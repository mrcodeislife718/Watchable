# Watchable TV — Programming Procurement

Watchable owns the customer platform. Programming suppliers provide authorized inventory.

## What we buy

- licensed live channel feeds;
- premium programming rights;
- selected sports rights;
- FAST/AVOD channels and VOD catalogs;
- EPG/metadata if supplied;
- explicit DVR/catch-up/ad inventory/device/concurrency terms.

## What we do not buy

- a replacement customer app;
- a white-label billing system;
- generic reseller panels that cannot document U.S. redistribution rights;
- open M3U/M3U8 lists as proof of permission.

## Supplier acceptance checklist

A supplier is not activated until we have written evidence covering:

1. U.S. distribution/redistribution rights for every supplied property.
2. Permitted delivery surfaces: web, mobile, smart TV and Watchable Streamer.
3. Per-subscriber fee, revenue share, minimum guarantee and escalator terms.
4. DVR/catch-up/start-over permissions.
5. Advertising inventory ownership and replacement rules.
6. Required DRM and output-protection rules.
7. Concurrent-stream and household/device limits.
8. Territory/geofencing requirements.
9. Reporting/audit obligations.
10. Termination and feed-continuity provisions.

## Current market leads to validate

- FurtherTV: streamlined rights-cleared FAST/AVOD content marketplace useful for VOD and FAST catalog depth.
- MwareTV/content-provider partnership: evidence that 200+ U.S. national/regional channels can be aggregated into a single operator agreement; validate whether programming-only supply is available without adopting its application stack.
- Big Pipe TV: licensed linear + FAST supplier for broadband/operator environments; useful if Watchable later operates or partners with an ISP, but current positioning appears operator/network focused.

The launch procurement team should prefer standardized rate-card/onboarding arrangements and use direct studio negotiations only for missing anchors.
